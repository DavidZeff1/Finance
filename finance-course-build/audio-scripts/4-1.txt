A central bank is a bank for banks and for the government. Not for you. You cannot open an account at the Federal Reserve. Its customers are commercial banks, which hold accounts there filled with reserves, the electronic cash banks use to settle payments with each other.

On top of that plumbing role, a central bank issues the currency, supervises banks, acts as lender of last resort during panics, and, the part markets obsess over, conducts monetary policy. Steering interest rates to keep the economy on track.

America's central bank is the Federal Reserve, created in nineteen thirteen, and it is deliberately weird in structure. A seven member Board of Governors in Washington, appointed by the President with staggered fourteen year terms, plus twelve regional Reserve Banks. New York, Chicago, San Francisco and so on. Rate decisions come from the F O M C, the Federal Open Market Committee. Seven governors, the New York Fed president, and four of the other eleven regional presidents on a rotating basis. Twelve votes, eight scheduled meetings a year.

Congress gave the Fed a dual mandate. Stable prices and maximum employment. Stable prices means inflation around two percent, measured by the P C E index. And those two goals often conflict, because cooling inflation usually means slowing the economy, which costs jobs. Monetary policy is the art of trading one against the other, in real time, with noisy data.

So why does everyone insist central banks be independent, free to set rates without political approval? Because politicians face elections, and cheap money feels great for about a year before the inflation bill arrives.

Watch the two natural experiments. Volcker's Fed, nineteen seventy nine to eighty three, independence used. Inflation hit fourteen point eight percent. Paul Volcker pushed rates to about twenty percent, the highest in American history. It worked and it hurt. Unemployment peaked at ten point eight percent, farmers drove tractors to Washington to blockade the building, and Congress drafted impeachment bills. Inflation fell to roughly three percent and stayed low for a generation. No elected official could have survived making that choice.

Now Turkey, twenty nineteen to twenty three, independence removed. The president, holding the view that high rates cause inflation, fired three central bank governors in under two years and had the policy rate cut from nineteen percent to eight and a half while inflation was accelerating. Official inflation peaked above eighty five percent, and the lira collapsed.

That asymmetry is the whole argument. Independence is a commitment device. A way to make a promise your future self cannot break.
